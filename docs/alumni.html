Alumni

Postdocs

Thomas Parmentier
Thomas studied communities of organisms living in ant nests (myrmecophiles), focusing on their dispersal, food webs and stability.

Léa Beaumelle
Léa used meta-analyses to study how chemical stressors and nutrients affect biodiversity and ecosystem functions such as litter decomposition.

Jurg Spaak (PhD student and postdoc)
Jurg worked on species coexistence theory, proposing a new definition of niche and fitness differences and extending it to higher-order and facilitative interactions.

Zhao Qinghua (PhD student and postdoc)
Qinghua studied how biodiversity, food web structure and temperature relate to the stability of aquatic food webs.

Javier Jarillo
Javier studied how stability scales across space and ecological levels in spatially structured community networks.

Jan Baert (PhD student and postdoc)
Jan studied how environmental stress changes the effects of biodiversity on ecosystem functioning, using experiments with diatom communities.

Devdutt Kulkarni

PhD students

Camille Carpentier (Master student and PhD student)
Camille studied the structure of food webs, including how the number of feeding links scales with the number of species.

Hélène Voahanginirina
Hélène studied how human activities in the Ankavia river basin (Madagascar) affect water quality, invertebrate and diatom communities, and organic matter decomposition.

Tessa de Bruin
Tessa studied how ciliate populations respond to climate change and pollution, linking demographic responses to changes in traits.

Mark Holmes
Mark studied the persistence, coexistence and adaptation of competitive communities subject to environmental change.

Daniel Oluwafemi Olusoji
Daniel studied the effects of temperature on intraspecific diversity in cyanobacteria and developed analytical tools to quantify it.

Sanne Van den Berg
Sanne studied how species traits can be used to predict the sensitivity of aquatic macroinvertebrates to chemicals.

Wouter Reyns
Wouter studied soil carbon sequestration in heathlands and how climate change affects it through its effects on fungi.

Jonathan De Raedt
Jonathan studied the combined effects of dispersal and chemical stress on the composition, diversity and productivity of marine microalgae communities.

Karel Viaene
Karel developed ecological models to make the risk assessment of chemicals more realistic, for example by accounting for species interactions.

Christoph Mensens
Christoph studied the effects of chemical stress across trophic levels at the base of marine food webs.

Lisette de Hoop
Lisette evaluated models of chemical exposure and effects in aquatic species, including the uptake and toxicity of oil constituents.
